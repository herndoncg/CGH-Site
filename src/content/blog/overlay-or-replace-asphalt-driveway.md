Most people call us thinking they need a whole new driveway. A good share of them do not. If the ground under the pavement is still solid, a new layer of asphalt on top, called an overlay, gives you a surface that looks and rides like new for a lot less money.

The trick is knowing which driveway you have. Here is what we look at.

## Signs an overlay will work

- **The surface is gray and rough, but firm.** Sun dries asphalt out over the years. Faded and weathered is a surface problem.
- **Cracks are single lines.** Cracks that run across or along the drive, with solid pavement on both sides, can be filled and covered.
- **Bad spots are few and small.** We cut those out, fix the base under them, and then overlay the whole thing.
- **Water mostly drains.** A shallow dip can be leveled before the overlay goes down.

## Signs the base has failed

- **Cracks in a tight web.** Pavement that looks like alligator skin has lost its support. New asphalt on top will crack the same way.
- **Soft or pumping areas.** If the pavement flexes under a truck, or mud works up through the cracks after rain, the base is wet and weak.
- **Deep ruts and sinking.** The ground below has moved, and a thin new layer will follow it down.
- **Large areas broken into chunks.** At that point there is nothing left to overlay.

## Why East Texas driveways fail from the bottom

Our red clay swells when it is wet and shrinks when it is dry. Add heavy spring rain and a driveway with nowhere for water to go, and the base turns to mud. That is why we spend so much time on grading and drainage. Asphalt is the part you see. The ground is the part that decides how long it lasts.

## What it means for cost

An overlay uses less material and skips the tear-out and haul-off, so it costs less than a rebuild. A rebuild costs more up front, but if the base is gone it is the only fix that holds.
