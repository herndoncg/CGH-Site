When a driveway fails early, the asphalt usually gets the blame. Most of the time the asphalt did its job. What was under it did not.

A paved surface has three parts, and you only ever see one of them.

## 1. Graded ground

Before anything else, the ground has to be shaped. Water needs a place to go that is not under your driveway. That means a slight slope or crown, fixing washouts and ruts, and packing down loose soil. This is dirt work, done with a dozer, skid steer or backhoe.

Skip it, and water sits under the pavement and softens everything above it.

## 2. Rock base

On top of the graded ground goes crushed rock, usually limestone, spread and compacted. The base spreads the weight of a vehicle over a wider area so the soil below does not give way.

Not every job needs the same amount. An old gravel drive that has been packed down for years may already be a good base. Bare clay, sandy spots and low wet areas need rock brought in. We price rock by the dump truck load, and only for the part of the job that needs it.

## 3. Asphalt

Last comes the hot mix: laid with a paver for an even thickness, then rolled while it is hot so it locks together. This is the wearing surface. It keeps water out of the base and gives you a smooth ride.

## How to tell what your site needs

Walk the area after a good rain and look for:

- Standing water or soft, spongy spots
- Ruts and channels where water has cut through
- Places where the gravel is thin and dirt shows through
